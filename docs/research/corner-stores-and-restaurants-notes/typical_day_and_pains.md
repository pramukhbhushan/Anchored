## How is each business structured: size, staff, revenue, offers, pricing and margins?

### Inferences
- `[Mine]` Firm size works as a stand-in for independents at the small end. Under-20 full-service firms number 159,252 against 159,644 locations, so nearly all are single-unit. They are 62% of full-service locations and 28% of receipts.
- `[Mine]` Under-5 convenience firms are 68% of no-fuel convenience locations and 39% of receipts.
- `[Mine]` Payroll share from the same workbook falls with size. Full service is 36.7% for all firms, 28.4% for firms under 20 and 23.0% for firms under 5. Limited service is 26.6% for all firms and 24.0% for firms under 20. The likely reason is unpaid owner and family labor, which the data cannot confirm.
- `[Mine]` On a $670,000 full-service shop at NRA's 2.8% median margin, profit is about $18,800. That median is not independent-specific.
- `[Mine]` Maryland's $45,000 mean lottery commission at 5.75% implies up to about $780,000 of lottery sales per retailer, skewed by chains and inflated by cashing commissions. Lottery may be counted only as commission in Census receipts, which I did not verify. A store's Census revenue and its register volume may therefore differ.
- `[Mine]` BizBuySell's median ask over its earnings multiple suggests owner earnings near $103,000, mixing medians across stores and including fuel. Treat it as a rough ceiling, not a typical owner income.

## What does a restaurant owner's day look like, hour by hour?

### Takeaway
I found no measured owner schedule. The table is a composite: timed points come from a vendor's manager template and one real owner interview, and the owner accounts mainly show that plans break. Whether each task is on paper, in a group chat or in a spreadsheet at independents is unmeasured, so the "Manual or not" column is mostly my inference.

### Cited findings
- [Restaurant365's manager day](https://www.restaurant365.com/blog/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-restaurant-manager/) `[Opened, Vendor]`: logbook 8:00, prior-day sales 8:30, deliveries ideally 8:00 to 10:00, labor check 10:00, pre-shift 10:45, lunch 11:00 to 2:00, invoices and vendor payments 2:00, inventory and next-day orders 3:00, transfers and waste log 4:00. It describes managers, not owners, and sells the software it shows.
- [NineGuides](https://nineguides.com/operations/day-in-life-restaurant-owner/) `[Opened, unnamed author, content marketing]` gives a recommended owner day rather than an observed one: 30 to 45 minutes reviewing the sales report, invoices, paid-outs and manager log; a briefing about 30 minutes before service; post-service review of reports, tips, labor and cash variances. It draws on coach David Scott Peters, who also [advises this routine](https://www.davidscottpeters.com/blog/the-ideal-daily-job-of-a-restaurant-owner/) `[Snippet]`.
- A real owner in Gastonia, NC writes that a plan lasts until something happens. His jobs include inventory, ordering, prep lists, schedules, hiring, payroll, advertising, maintenance and working the line, sushi and expo. He restocks by driving to a Food Lion, and interviews candidates himself. He gives few clock times. A dinner rush at 6:45 pulled him into service. [Bep-Pa owner essay](https://bep-pa.com/running-a-family-restaurant/) `[Opened]`
- Another owner runs a split week. At her bagel shop she wakes at 4:35, drives about an hour, bakes to open, makes dough, trains and steps out for computer work until she leaves at 11:00 to noon. Weekend restaurant shifts run about 7:00 to 4:30 with constant task switching. Admin, invoices, payroll and ordering times are not given. [Sykes interview, May 2026](https://lesleysykes.substack.com/p/day-in-the-life-4-restaurant-owner) `[Opened]`
- A break from 3:00 to 6:00 and a close around midnight appear in a [general chef's answer](https://www.careervillage.org/questions/252245/what-is-a-regular-day-in-the-life-of-a-chef-like) `[Snippet, low authority]`.
- 68% of calls land 11:00 to 2:00 or 5:00 to 9:00, per Maple's call data `[Prior note, Vendor]`.

### Typical day table, full-service shop open for lunch and dinner

The order is my composite `[Mine]`. "Timed" means a source gives the clock time. Otherwise the placement is mine.

| Time | Task | Manual or not | Why it hurts | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7:00 to 8:00 | Unlock, walk-through, check overnight messages and staff gaps | Manual; messages likely by text, unmeasured | A no-show cook reshuffles the day before it starts | Placement mine |
| 8:00 to 8:45 | Read yesterday's sales, invoices, paid-outs and log | POS report plus paper or manual review | Recommended, but likely skipped when the owner works a station | Timed, R365 and NineGuides |
| 8:00 to 10:00 | Receive deliveries, check against invoice | Manual, hand check, paper invoice | Done during prep, so shorts and price changes pass unseen | Timed, R365 |
| 9:00 to 10:45 | Prep, prep lists, cash drawers | Manual | Owner labor fills the gap | R365, Bep-Pa; times mine |
| 10:00 | Labor check | POS or by eye | Small shops have little slack | Timed, R365 |
| 10:45 | Pre-shift huddle | Verbal | Specials and shorts passed on by word of mouth | Timed, R365 |
| 11:00 to 2:00 | Lunch rush, phone and app orders | Mixed; tablets and phone | Peak calls compete with the line | Timed, R365; Maple |
| 2:00 | Check invoices, pay vendors | Manual or spreadsheet; accounting software unmeasured | No time to compare each line with the last price | Timed, R365 |
| 3:00 | Count stock, place next-day orders | Manual count, supplier portal, phone or text | Over-ordering and stockouts | Timed, R365 |
| 3:00 to 5:00 | Afternoon lull: hiring, repairs, errands, schedule | Manual; schedule tool use unmeasured | Lull is taken up by whatever broke | CareerVillage, Bep-Pa; placement mine |
| 4:00 | Log waste and transfers | Often skipped, inferred | Without a log, variance cannot be explained | Timed, R365 |
| 5:00 to 9:00+ | Dinner rush, owner on line or floor | Manual | Owner cannot do admin; guests expect them | Maple, Bep-Pa |
| 9:00 to midnight | Close, cash-out, tip-out, deposit | POS plus cash count; paper unmeasured | Tip errors carry wage risk; cash variance found late | NineGuides, CareerVillage |
| After close or day off | Schedule, payroll inputs, reviews, app disputes, books | Mixed, phone at night, inferred | Fragmented; 3.7 hours a week on scheduling, vendor only | Prior note; placement mine |

### Inferences
- `[Mine]` The real gap is between R365's clean 2:00 invoice slot and the owner accounts, in which the owner is pulled into service. Cards an owner can approve between tasks fit the owner's day better than a block of admin.
- `[Mine]` The morning delivery and the 2:00 invoice review are hours apart, which means a photo at the door could feed a later check.

- Share using paper, group chat or spreadsheets for each task: not found.

## What does a corner store owner's day look like, hour by hour?

### Takeaway
Owner accounts confirm long opening hours run by family shifts, daily buying trips and constant counter duty. Cash, lottery settlement and paperwork timing are not documented anywhere I could open. Those rows are inference.

### Cited findings
- Shifts: one SoHo deli runs 7am to 5pm by one brother and 5pm to 1am by another, seven days, with their father also in the business. [The Fader, 2016](https://www.thefader.com/2016/06/27/how-6-bodega-owners-make-an-honest-living-in-nyc) `[Opened]`
- A Sunset Park owner works seven days, often 14 to 15 hours a day, per the reporter's account in [Fortune via Yahoo, 2017](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/two-ex-googlers-want-kill-201906847.html?_guc_consent_skip=1552840917) `[Opened]`.
- A Passaic owner said he opened at 5am and closed around 11pm. The same report says 15-hour days for six years, so the figures do not fully agree. [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/world/powerball-winner-pedro-quezada-will-close-bodega-unsure-how-hell-spend-his-millions) `[Snippet]`
- West Baltimore: Tae Soon and Kevin Lee have run a store since 1994, more than eight hours a day and seven days a week until recently cutting back to take Tuesdays off. Kevin shops daily at Walmart, Costco and B Green Wholesale, then stocks and wipes down dairy. Tae Soon runs checkout and chats with regulars. Customers are buzzed in. [Baltimore Banner, Dec 2022](https://www.thebanner.com/community/local-news/lees-mini-market-west-baltimore-WYBL6RZYM5GY5LSQFG2VH5R6IE/) `[Opened]`
- B Green's Monroe Street cash-and-carry lists weekday hours of 6am to 4pm. [Green Valley Marketplace, B Green locations](https://www.bgreenco.net/locations/) `[Snippet]`
- Stock comes from several distributors: alcohol, baked goods, dairy, candy and snacks. Owners sometimes buy at other stores and mark up. [MEL, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/the-quick-and-ubiquitous-economics-of-bodegas) `[Opened]`
- A vendor says a common failure is that owners sign the driver's handheld and put the paper invoice in a drawer, so errors go undetected. [Fintech, Vendor](https://fintech.com/blog/best-practices-for-checking-in-vendor-deliveries-at-the-back-door) `[Snippet]`
- Cigarette price changes have no fixed schedule. Manufacturers set list prices wholesalers pay, and increases usually pass to retail. One Altria increase came 84 days after the last. The [CSP coverage](https://www.cspdailynews.com/tobacco/altria-increases-cigarette-prices) is old `[Snippet]`. Altria's 2026 outlook still relies on [price hikes](https://www.itiger.com/news/2607080103) `[Snippet]`.
- Lottery settlement: Maryland's cadence and handbook were not found. Other states show the shape. Texas bills retailers weekly by EFT [FAQ](https://www.texaslottery.com/export/sites/lottery/Documents/retailers/FAQ_Basic_Ret_Policies__Practices_100826_web.pdf), and Oklahoma publishes a [scratcher reconciliation guide](https://content.lottery.ok.gov/retailer-guides/misc/scratcher-reconciliation.pdf) and settles packs at activation or after 21 days. All `[Snippet]`, none Maryland.
- Maryland's age check to 30, behind-the-counter rule, fines and 1 to 2 weekly deliveries are in the existing note `[Prior note]`.

**Restaurants**

Every failed owner mentioned the burden of the time commitment at some point, and five said they closed when no longer willing to make family sacrifices. One owner refused to add lunch because dinner already demanded enough from his family. An interviewee said: "Family and spousal support is essential for the success of a restaurant." The paper gives no hours figure.
- 60 to 80 hours for [franchisees](https://fddiq.com/learn/franchise-ownership/franchisee-day-in-the-life) `[Snippet]`. 40-plus and often 60-plus for managers, from [R365](https://www.restaurant365.com/blog/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-restaurant-manager/) `[Opened, Vendor]`. Neither describes independent owners.
- A consultancy blog says 74% of restaurant owners work over 60 hours, crediting NRA. The search could not trace it to NRA, so do not use it. [Masterestaurant](https://masterestaurant.com/EN/autonomous-restaurant-business-without-the-owner-myth-vs-reality.html) `[Snippet]`

**All small business, weak**
- 33% over 50 hours and another 25% over 60 in a [2006 reader poll](https://www.inc.com/news/articles/200604/overworked.html) `[Snippet]`. 63% over 50 hours in a [PR-trade survey](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-news/pr-news-trends/time-management-new-survey-reveals-biz-owners-spending-time-theyd-rather-spend/) with method unchecked `[Snippet]`.
- BLS sector hours near 25 to 26 a week [cover employees, not owners](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag72.htm) `[Snippet]`.

### Inferences

- `[Mine]` A separate admin share probably misstates the problem. Admin happens between customers and late at night, which diaries would need to capture. The manager figure of 8 to 10 hours a week is vendor-only and from the existing note.

## What does each pain cost per year per store?

### Takeaway
Few of these are measured for independents. Ranked by annual dollars on a modeled store, the biggest recurring costs are outside fees and food cost in a restaurant, and shrink and card fees in a corner store. Rare license or SNAP loss and wage claims are larger events but not annual. The one traceable invoice-error figure implies only about $700 to $1,100 a year on a small restaurant, far below what vendor pitches suggest. Price creep could be far larger but has no measurement.

### Cited findings: measured or reported
- **Invoice errors.** Consolidated Concepts reviewed over 11,000 invoices from 400 restaurants in 2015. 35% had at least one overcharge, and typical overcharges were about 1% of invoice total. It sells supply chain services, and the data is old. [FSR, Dec 2015](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/finance/overcharges-continue-show-restaurant-invoices) `[Opened, Vendor]`. [Food Service Director](https://www.foodservicedirector.com/news-trends/analysis-35-college-food-invoices-have-overcharges) is linked to the 35% figure in search, and another summary credits Buyers Edge with 1.5% `[Snippet]`. Supy says nearly 1 in 4 invoices differ from the expected price, skewing against the buyer ([Supy](https://supy.io/the-spread), `[Snippet, Vendor]`). The "39% of invoices" figure [credited to IOFM](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/how-to-catch-restaurant-vendor-price-creep) was not traced, so do not use it.
- **Theft.** POS monitoring across 392 locations in 39 states cut theft 22% and raised revenue about $2,982 a week, about 7%. The press release does not say chain or independent, and gives no theft dollars. [Newswise, 2013, on Pierce, Snow and McAfee](https://www.newswise.com/articles/it-monitoring-effective-in-deterring-restaurant-fraud) `[Opened]`
- **Food waste.** 114 restaurants in 12 countries saved about $7 for every $1 invested in kitchen waste programs. Multinational, no independent split, and no percent of purchases in what I saw. [Champions 12.3](https://champions123.org/release-new-report-finds-restaurants-save-significant-money-fighting-food-waste) `[Snippet]`
- **Spoilage proxy, supermarkets only.** Stores estimate total shrink at 2.1%, deli 5.5%, produce 5.2%, per [a retail food waste survey](https://hackingfoodwaste.nmsu.edu/documents/Retail-Food-Waste-Executive-Summary.pdf). USDA-linked research has fresh fruit at 11.4 to 12.6% and vegetables at 9.7 to 11.6% by weight. [Farms.com on USDA](https://m.farms.com/news/supermarket-shrink-varies-by-type-of-fresh-fruit-and-vegetable-109299.aspx). All `[Snippet]`. Corner store spoilage: not found.
- **Retail shrink.** 1.6% of sales for all retail in NRF's fiscal 2022 survey, with no authoritative later rate, per [a retail theft summary](https://surveillant.ai/guides/retail-theft-statistics) `[Snippet, Vendor]`. Convenience-store claims of 2 to 4% are [unsourced](https://envysion.com/industries/convenience-store-industries/convenience-store-trends-and-how-to-stay-ahead/) `[Snippet, Vendor]`. Vendor and delivery errors at 5 to 10% of shrink appear on [a weak page](https://gas-station-compliance-hub.pages.dev/operations/c-store-inventory-management-reduce-shrink-optimize-margins) `[Snippet]`. The 1.6% and $2,868-a-store figure in the existing note stays unverified.
- **Card fees.** Vendor guides say 2 to 3.5% of card volume, cards at 70 to 85% of transactions, and sometimes 3.3 to 4% with tips and tax. [Back of House](https://backofhouse.io/resources/what-you-need-to-know-about-restaurant-processing-fees-part-1), [PaymentCollect](https://www.paymentcollect.com/restaurant-credit-card-processing-fees/), [SolveCompare](https://solvecompare.com/restaurant-credit-card-processing-fees-2026/) `[Snippet, Vendor]`. No non-vendor rate for independents was found.
- **Bookkeeping.** $100 to $300 a month for small cafes and restaurants, $300 to $700 for mid-size, or $500 to $1,500 on another page from the same vendor. [RemoteBooksOnline](https://www.remotebooksonline.com/blog/what-is-the-average-cost-of-bookkeeping-services-for-cafes-and-restaurants) and [its industry guide](https://www.remotebooksonline.com/blog/bookkeeping-cost-by-industry) `[Snippet, Vendor]`. Annual CPA and tax cost, and corner store figures: not found.
- **SNAP penalties.** Federal rule text: permanent disqualification for trafficking; 5, 3 or 1 years or 6 months for other first sanctions, doubled on repeat. A 1-year sanction includes accepting SNAP as payment for goods sold to a household on credit. A hardship penalty is monthly redemptions x 10% x months of disqualification. A trafficking penalty is monthly redemptions x 10% x 60, or x 120 if the largest trafficking transaction was $100 or more, with caps. [7 CFR 278.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/278.6) `[Opened]`. This reconciles the 6x and 12x formulas found in law firm snippets. The trafficking cap was $60,161 in 2018 per [OFW Law](https://ofwlaw.com/snap-trafficking-and-other-civil-monetary-penalties-edge-higher/) `[Opened]`. Current cap: not found. Nearly 95% of permanent disqualifications involved convenience and small grocery stores in [an older FNS summary](https://ofwlaw.com/?p=8095) `[Snippet]`.
- **Lottery.** Maryland commissions fell from $220.2M to $195.7M after the June 2024 cut, across about 4,300 retailers `[Prior note]`.
- **Wage claims.** DOL-publicized restaurant cases of $61,568, $107,300 and $200,137 `[Prior note, Snippet]`.
- **Tobacco.** Maryland $500 first fine, suspension up to 90 days on a second violation; FDA up to $20,678 in early 2024 `[Prior note]`.
- **No-shows and missed calls.** Vendor data only: 2.33 to 3.7% no-show; call miss rates of 9.0 to 40.1% by format; Revmo's $73,000 per quick-service location has no inputs `[Prior note, Vendor]`.
- **Turnover.** Hard cost $2,706 per hourly worker, $11,940 per manager; quits 3.5% a month in August 2026 `[Prior note]`. Total separations ran 5.70% in June 2025 in the [FRED series](https://fred-trends.vercel.app/trend/JTU7200TSR) `[Snippet]`. No independent split.

### Inferences
- `[Mine]` Invoice checking as a stand-alone money story is thin on the only traceable number. Its better claim is catching price creep, plus a frequent reason to open the app. Test this before quoting any annual saving.
- `[Mine]` Many large numbers are fees or owner-chosen channels, not errors. A card can show them and propose a change, but the recoverable share is unmeasured and likely much smaller than the totals.
- `[Mine]` For corner stores, the sharpest cards are about losing a revenue line, not trimming a cost. The credit-tab rule is a concrete example: a tab repaid with EBT can trigger a 1-year disqualification, and owners may not connect the two.
- `[Mine]` Pierce's 7% revenue effect came from locations averaging about $2.2M a year. It should not be scaled to a family shop.

## What do owners say they would never hand to software?

### Takeaway
I found no owner statement or survey that lists tasks they would never hand over, so no quote is offered. Available evidence shows caution about prices, schedules and customer relationships, but mostly from vendors, chains and advice pieces. Treat the list below as a hypothesis for owner interviews.

### Cited findings
- A multi-unit franchise operator cut annual pricing analysis from five days to one or two hours using ChatGPT. When tried on scheduling, his manager caught missing staffing rules and still reviews and enters the schedule. Not an independent, and sourced from OpenAI's account. [AI Adopters on the OpenAI case](https://aiadopters.club/p/before-you-change-a-price-check-the) `[Snippet]`
- A PAR-cited guide advises keeping menu and pricing changes under chef or manager approval until the system proves reliable. This is advice, not owner opinion. [The Restaurant HQ](https://www.therestauranthq.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-in-the-restaurant-industry-diners-warm-up-evidence-thin/) `[Snippet, vendor-linked]`
- Harri, 600 operators: nearly 40% see scheduling and labor as the biggest AI opportunity, and about one in five cannot envision a use. [Hospitality Technology](https://hospitalitytech.com/restaurant-operators-know-where-ai-belongs-why-arent-brands-listening) `[Snippet, Vendor; direct fetch returned 403]`
- NRA, 212 operators: 38.6% use AI tools, 3.2% companywide and 20% in small pilots. Top concerns are complexity 46.1%, cost 43.5% and customer acceptance 41.9%. [Same results page](https://www.therestauranthq.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-in-the-restaurant-industry-diners-warm-up-evidence-thin/) `[Snippet]`
- Baltimore owners resist changing prices or running discounts because customers react badly, and will not stock new items unless enough customers ask. [Baltimore interviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5702771) `[Opened]`
- A Manhattan store owner asks customers about products before stocking and adds items gradually. [MEL, 2020](https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/the-quick-and-ubiquitous-economics-of-bodegas) `[Opened]`
- The most successful owners in Parsa's sample said they did not believe in advertising or promotions, and instead described community and customer relationships. Sample is from 2005. [Parsa et al.](https://econ.ucsb.edu/%7Etedb/Courses/Ec1F07/restaurantsfail.pdf) `[Opened]`
- Face-to-face regulars are part of the offer: the Banner shows the owner chatting about customers' families and letting some pay later. [Baltimore Banner](https://www.thebanner.com/community/local-news/lees-mini-market-west-baltimore-WYBL6RZYM5GY5LSQFG2VH5R6IE/) `[Opened]`

### Inferences
Hypotheses `[Mine]`, not owner statements:
- Setting a shelf or menu price without approval.
- Deciding who gets a tab, or calling in one.
- Hiring, firing or changing a person's schedule by itself.
- Paying a supplier, or moving cash, without approval.
- Disputing with a long-time supplier rep without the owner's knowledge.
- Replying as the owner to a regular, or running a discount without approval.
- Anything that answers a license inspector or tax agency.

The one-tap approval model fits these if the card drafts and the owner decides. Code should compute prices and rule results, with the model explaining them.

### Gaps
- Direct owner statements from r/restaurantowners and r/smallbusiness: tools refused. Someone needs to read these by hand.
- A survey of independent owners on off-limits tasks: not found. Three to five owner interviews would give better evidence than further searching.
